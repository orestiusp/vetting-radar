"This month I would like to talk about one of the major activities currently underway in OCIMF – the development of the fifth edition of our flagship publication Mooring Equipment Guidelines (MEG)."
Director's Log
When the fourth edition of MEG was published in 2018, it marked a significant step forward in the approach to mooring safety. It considered multiple mooring components working together as part of a mooring system and it introduced the implications of human factors into mooring.
Now, as OCIMF works with dozens of industry partners and stakeholders to develop MEG5, our responsibility is to ensure the revised publication incorporates not only developments in standards and technology, but also the realities faced by those working with these mooring systems.
Meaningful industry engagement is essential to achieving this ambition. We need to hear from those who design, manufacture, operate and maintain mooring systems, including the seafarers and shore personnel who use the guidance in practice. Their experience and lessons learned will help the MEG5 Working Group test assumptions, resolve ambiguity and focus on the areas where improved guidance is most needed.
I would like to thank those who are already working with us to improve this important aspect of maritime safety, and I encourage everyone to contribute to MEG5 by providing feedback through the
Survey for the revision of Mooring Equipment Guidelines
.
Don Davis
Managing Director OCIMF

OCIMF launches new SIRE Cat-1 inspector training programme
OCIMF launched its revised training course for SIRE Cat-1 new inspectors in London 7-11 September 2026.
The duration of the new training course is seven weeks, with a mix of online, asynchronous and face-to-face, synchronous modules. The course is built on a workshop style of delivery and demands a greater level of interaction and attention from the candidates.
The course takes aspiring inspectors step-by-step through the basics of becoming a SIRE Cat-1 inspector. It provides candidates with a comprehensive understanding of the processes, procedures, practices and human factor skills required to successfully prepare for and complete a SIRE 2.0 vessel inspection and submit an inspection report that accurately reflects the condition of the vessel and its operation.
The course includes assessments at various stages to check candidates understand and have retained the information given to maintain the standards expected by OCIMF members and users of the SIRE inspection programme.
The candidates attending the first course represented 14 OCIMF member organisations. It was delivered by OCIMF’s Training and Accreditation Manager and two Quality Assessors and was well received by attendees.

Industry feedback sought for revision of Mooring Equipment Guidelines
Work is underway on the next edition of the Mooring Equipment Guidelines (MEG) and OCIMF is seeking feedback from across the industry to support the revision.
A new survey is now open for those involved with mooring systems and equipment to share practical experience, lessons learned and industry perspectives. Responses will be reviewed by the MEG Working Group and used solely to support the development of the revised publication. Information provided will not be shared outside the MEG revision process without permission.
The survey takes approximately 20–30 minutes to complete and questions may be skipped. The survey closes on 23 October 2026.

PIANC Working Group on remote operations
Barge Adviser Ton Mol represented OCIMF at the fifth meeting of PIANC Working Group 262 for Remote Operated Inland Waterway Operations.
The working group, which met at the Seafar Remote Operations Centre in Antwerp on 10 September, is compiling a report giving recommendations on remote operations for Inland Waterway Authorities (worldwide). The report will be published during the Smart Rivers Conference in Indonesia in June 2027.
Joint meeting of inspection bodies
OCIMF was invited to join the three-day meeting organised by the technical body of the Rhine Commission, CESNI.
The meeting, held in Rotterdam from 15–17 September, was attended by 110 representatives of classification societies, member states and national authorities. It had three objectives: to inform the experts about the main developments in international regulatory frameworks; to exchange the experiences of inspection bodies and approved classification societies in applying technical requirements; and to draft proposals for interpretations in a coordinated manner.

OCIMF engagement at Kuala Lumpur OVID User Forum and Oil & Gas Asia
OCIMF was delighted to bring together the offshore marine community in Kuala Lumpur for a successful OVID User Forum followed by participation at Oil & Gas Asia.

IMO news
12th session of the Sub-committee on Carriage of Cargoes and Containers (CCC 12)
CCC 12 took place at the IMO headquarters from 14 to 18 September 2026. Preceded by an intersessional working group on alternative fuels, the Sub-committee achieved the following:
Finalised the revised guidelines for safety of ships using methyl/ethyl alcohol as fuels. These contain functional requirements for shipboard installations to ensure that their safety philosophy and reliability are equivalent to conventional oil-fuelled machinery systems.
Agreed on the scope of guidelines for ships using Onboard Carbon Capture and Storage Systems (OCCS). The intent is to remain technology-neutral and safety-focused. Environmental performance and carbon accounting, i.e. aspects that do not concern safety, are considered out of scope.
Next steps include the development of guidelines for OCCS and low-flashpoint oil fuels, and revision of guidelines for fuel cell power installations. These will be developed intersessionally, through correspondence groups reporting to CCC 13 in 2027.
IMO Secretary-General briefing to Member States and NGOs
The Secretary-General, Arsenio Dominguez, has been updating Member States and consultative NGOs on the work of the IMO Secretariat through regular face-to-face briefings.
Ahead of World Maritime Day on 24 September, he expressed deep concerns over innocent civilian seafarer lives lost in the attacks on commercial ships in recent months and highlighted the importance of upholding the regulatory framework keeping shipping and seafarers safe. He spoke about the impact of conflict on families, the maritime supply chain and the wider economy, urging all delegates to continue to raise these matters with respective governments and international forums with a view to finding solutions to current regional conflicts.
